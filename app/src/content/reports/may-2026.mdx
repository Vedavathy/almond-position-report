## Market Update

The Almond Board of California released the May 2026 Position Report on June 10, 2026. This report represents the ninth installment for the 2025–2026 crop year, covering activity through the end of May and providing an updated picture of where the industry stands as the current season enters its final months before the new harvest begins.

## Receipts

For-the-month receipts in May came in at 2.53 million lbs., down 10.1% compared to the 2.81 million lbs. recorded in May of last year. At this late stage of the crop year, monthly receipt volumes are naturally modest, reflecting the tail end of what has already been delivered from the 2025 harvest. The slight decline is not a cause for concern and is broadly consistent with the cadence of prior seasons as remaining handler inventory stabilizes heading into summer.

On a year-to-date basis, total receipts reached 2.691 billion lbs., compared to 2.712 billion lbs. through the same period in the prior crop year, a difference of down 0.8% year over year. The narrow gap between the two crop years confirms that the 2025 California harvest, while marginally smaller than 2024, delivered a substantial and competitive supply to the market. The cumulative receipts figure provides a solid foundation for meeting demand through the balance of the crop year.

## Shipments

Total shipments for the month of May reached 217.36 million lbs., up 2.6% versus the 211.76 million lbs. shipped in May of last year. This is an encouraging result, demonstrating continued healthy demand from buyers across channels and geographies. On a year-to-date basis, shipments stand at 2.213 billion lbs., compared to 2.263 billion lbs. through May of the prior crop year, placing the cumulative pace down 2.2% year over year. While the YTD figure trails last season, the positive monthly momentum in May suggests that the market is finding its footing after a more measured start to the year.

Domestic demand has remained a consistent contributor to overall shipment performance, with buyers in the United States continuing to incorporate almonds across a wide range of food and ingredient applications. Retail and foodservice channels have both shown resilience, and there are no signs of significant demand destruction at the domestic level. The steady pace of domestic purchasing reflects the enduring appeal of almonds as a versatile, nutritious ingredient with broad consumer acceptance.

On the export side, international markets continue to absorb meaningful volumes of California almonds, with key regions in Europe, South Asia, and the Middle East maintaining active procurement programs. While the cumulative YTD shipment gap versus last year is partly attributable to earlier softness in certain export lanes, the recovery visible in the May monthly figures suggests that overseas buyers are becoming more engaged as the season progresses. Price competitiveness and the global reputation of California almonds remain important factors supporting continued international demand.

## Sales and Commitments

New sales recorded for May totaled 124.94 million lbs., up a notable 21.5% compared to the 102.80 million lbs. in new sales reported for May of last year. This is one of the more meaningful data points in this report, as it reflects active forward buying from customers who are securing supply ahead of the new crop. The strength in new sales indicates that buyers have conviction about incorporating California almonds into their procurement plans and are not waiting on the sidelines for further price signals.

Total commitments across the current crop year now stand at 438.26 million lbs., up 8.9% versus the 402.31 million lbs. committed at the same point last season. Uncommitted inventory, meanwhile, sits at 469.54 million lbs., down 5.2% compared to the 495.22 million lbs. that remained uncommitted through May of the prior crop year. The combination of rising commitments and falling uncommitted inventory is a constructive signal, suggesting that the pipeline is tightening in an orderly fashion and that available supply is being absorbed at a reasonable pace relative to the season's timeline.

## In a Nutshell

The May 2026 Position Report presents a broadly balanced picture of the almond market as the 2025–2026 crop year moves into its final stretch. Receipts are essentially in line with last year on a cumulative basis, monthly shipments have turned positive on a year-over-year comparison, and new sales are running well ahead of the prior season's pace. Taken together, these indicators suggest a market that is functioning with reasonable efficiency and building toward a more fully committed inventory position before the 2026 harvest arrives.

The most encouraging development in this report is the 21.5% year-over-year increase in monthly sales, which reflects genuine buying interest from customers rather than a passive market waiting for conditions to shift. The corresponding decline in uncommitted inventory, down 5.2% versus last May, reinforces the view that supply and demand are moving into closer alignment. Handlers and growers alike should find reassurance in the fact that the industry is entering the new harvest season with a tighter, more committed book of business than at the comparable point last year.

Looking ahead, attention will turn to early estimates for the 2026 California crop, as well as to the pace of shipments through June and July. If the monthly shipment momentum seen in May carries forward, the YTD gap to last season should continue to narrow. The market enters this transition period with a fundamentally sound demand profile, and the data from this report supports a measured sense of confidence about the remainder of the crop year and the season to come.

## Additional Insights

The May 2026 position data reflects a market navigating a complex balance between tempered year-to-date volume and strengthening forward commitments. The 8.9% year-over-year increase in commitments to 438.26 million pounds is a meaningful signal that buyer confidence is building as the 2025-26 crop year moves into its final quarter, suggesting handlers and international buyers are positioning ahead of new-crop availability rather than waiting on price discovery. This forward-buying behavior aligns with patterns observed when uncommitted inventory contracts — the 5.2% decline in uncommitted stocks to 469.54 million pounds indicates the market is tightening in a measured way, without the excess carryover that suppressed prices in prior seasons. The single-month shipment recovery of 2.6% in May, against a still-negative year-to-date trend of -2.2%, may reflect stabilizing export demand after earlier softness driven by currency headwinds and macroeconomic caution in key destinations including the European Union and India.

On the global supply side, the 2026 Australian crop, harvested earlier in the calendar year, delivered a moderate yield that largely met trade expectations without generating significant surplus volumes that would pressure California pricing. Select Harvest and other major Australian producers have indicated continued investment in orchard maturation, meaning Southern Hemisphere supply will remain a structural factor in global almond trade, though not at volumes sufficient to displace California's dominant market position in the near term. Meanwhile, the Almond Board of California's crop estimate for the 2025-26 season reflected acreage that has largely stabilized following the multiyear expansion period, and early bloom and set reports from the 2026-27 growing season were generally favorable across the San Joaquin Valley following adequate winter chill hours and a manageable spring frost window. USDA NASS bearing acreage data continues to show that the rapid expansion phase has decelerated, which lends longer-term support to the supply-demand equilibrium that the current commitment figures are beginning to reflect.

Export market dynamics remain a central consideration heading into the summer months, when new-crop contracting activity typically accelerates. The INC's global nut consumption data has continued to show resilient long-run demand growth for tree nuts, even as near-term consumer spending in Europe has been constrained by persistent inflationary pressures and softened discretionary budgets. Indian import volumes, which had been a point of concern through portions of 2025 due to rupee volatility and domestic policy uncertainty, appear to be finding firmer footing, and the reduction in uncommitted inventory suggests California handlers are not overly exposed to spot-market risk as harvest approaches.
