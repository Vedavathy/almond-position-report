## Market Update

The Almond Board of California released the May 2026 Position Report on June 10, 2026, covering handler movement and use through May 31, 2026. This is the ninth report of the 2025–2026 crop year, reflecting the most complete picture of the season to date as the industry moves through the final months before the 2026 harvest begins.

## Receipts

For-the-month receipts in May came in at 2.53 million lbs., down 10.1% compared to 2.81 million lbs. in May of last year. At this point in the season, monthly receipt figures are modest by nature, as the bulk of the crop was delivered to handlers in the early months of the crop year, and this softness should not be read as a supply concern. Year-to-date receipts through May stand at 2.691 billion lbs., down just 0.8% versus the 2.712 billion lbs. recorded through the same period last year. The near-parity in cumulative receipts confirms that the 2025 crop came in largely in line with the prior season, leaving the supply base on reasonably stable footing as the industry approaches the transition to a new harvest.

## Shipments

Total shipments in May reached 217.36 million lbs., up 2.6% compared to 211.76 million lbs. in May of last year, representing one of the more encouraging data points in this report. Month-over-month momentum in outbound volume is a constructive signal, particularly at this stage of the crop year when buyers are working through near-term needs ahead of a seasonal slowdown. On a cumulative basis, however, year-to-date shipments of 2.213 billion lbs. remain down 2.2% against the 2.263 billion lbs. shipped through May 2024–2025, a gap that the market will need to continue closing in the weeks ahead.

Domestic demand has been a consistent contributor to shipment performance throughout this crop year, and May's figures suggest that trend remains intact. Retail and industrial channels have shown steady purchasing behavior, supported by relatively stable pricing and good product availability across the size and specification range. The domestic channel continues to serve as a reliable foundation for overall movement.

On the export side, May shipments contributed to a global demand picture that, while slightly below prior-year pace on a cumulative basis, is showing signs of improvement. Key markets in Europe and Asia have been absorbing California almonds at competitive price levels, and the uptick in for-the-month volume suggests that overseas buyers may be returning with greater urgency as crop-year inventories remain available. Any further acceleration in export activity over the coming months would go a long way toward bringing year-to-date totals back toward parity with last season.

## Sales and Commitments

New sales booked in May totaled 124.94 million lbs., up a notable 21.5% compared to 102.80 million lbs. in May of last year. This is a meaningful improvement and points to strengthening buyer confidence, as purchasers appear increasingly willing to commit to forward volume at current price levels. The year-over-year gain in monthly sales activity is one of the more positive developments in this report and suggests that market engagement is picking up as the crop year progresses.

Total commitments outstanding stand at 438.26 million lbs., up 8.9% versus the 402.31 million lbs. reported at this time last year. Uncommitted inventory, meanwhile, sits at 469.54 million lbs., down 5.2% from 495.22 million lbs. in the comparable prior-year period. The combination of higher commitments and lower uncommitted inventory is a constructive pairing, indicating that available supply is being absorbed more effectively than it was at this point in the 2024–2025 crop year. Handlers and buyers alike appear to be navigating the back end of the season with a clearer sense of where the market stands.

## In a Nutshell

The May 2026 Position Report presents a generally constructive picture for the California almond industry, with several indicators moving in a favorable direction even as year-to-date shipments remain modestly behind prior-year pace. The 2.6% gain in for-the-month shipments, the 21.5% increase in monthly sales, and the 8.9% rise in commitments all point to a market that is gaining traction. At the same time, the 0.8% decline in year-to-date receipts is negligible in practical terms, confirming that this crop year's supply base is comparable to last season's.

The most important forward-looking signal in this report is the reduction in uncommitted inventory, which at 469.54 million lbs. is 5.2% below where it stood last May. With roughly one month remaining in the current crop year before the 2026 harvest begins arriving, the pace of sales and shipment activity will be closely watched. A continued uptick in export demand, combined with the strong sales momentum visible in May, would support a healthy transition into the new crop without significant carryover pressure weighing on the market.

Looking ahead, the industry's attention will naturally shift toward early harvest estimates and initial reports on crop development in the San Joaquin and Sacramento Valleys. Growers and handlers are entering this transition period with reasonable inventory levels and an improving demand environment, which provides a solid base from which to begin the new season. The fundamentals, on balance, support measured confidence as the 2025–2026 crop year draws toward a close.
