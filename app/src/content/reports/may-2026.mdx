## Market Update

The Almond Board of California released the May 2026 Position Report in June 2026, covering movement and inventory data through the end of May. This is the ninth position report of the 2025–2026 crop year, offering a near-complete picture of how the season has performed as it approaches its final months.

## Receipts

For-the-month receipts in May came in at 2.53 million lbs., down 10.1% compared to the 2.81 million lbs. recorded in May of last year. At this late stage of the crop year, monthly receipt volumes are naturally modest, and the year-over-year variance, while meaningful in percentage terms, reflects a relatively small absolute difference in pounds delivered to handlers. The more telling figure is the cumulative one: year-to-date receipts now stand at 2.691 billion lbs., down just 0.8% versus the 2.712 billion lbs. received through the same point last season. That narrow gap suggests the current crop has tracked closely with last year's supply profile, and the final receipt total is unlikely to shift materially from where it stands today.

## Shipments

Total shipments for May reached 217.36 million lbs., up 2.6% compared to the 211.76 million lbs. shipped in May 2025. That is an encouraging result for a month that typically reflects how well the market has absorbed supply as the season winds down. On a cumulative basis, however, year-to-date shipments of 2.213 billion lbs. remain modestly behind last year's 2.263 billion lbs. pace, a decline of 2.2% year over year.

Domestic demand has continued to provide a steady foundation for overall shipment performance, even as trade dynamics and consumer purchasing patterns have evolved throughout the year. The pace of domestic movement has helped offset some of the softness seen in other channels and reflects ongoing category engagement from food manufacturers and retailers alike.

On the export side, international markets have remained active, and the May shipment result suggests that overseas buyers are continuing to draw down their positions and replenish stocks. While cumulative export volumes have faced headwinds from currency pressures and competitive sourcing in select regions, the month-over-month improvement in total shipments points to resilient underlying demand across key markets. How export commitments convert into physical shipments over the remaining weeks of the crop year will be worth watching closely.

## Sales and Commitments

New sales recorded in May totaled 124.94 million lbs., up a notable 21.5% compared to the 102.80 million lbs. in new sales reported for the same month last year. That level of forward buying activity this deep into the crop year reflects genuine commercial interest and suggests that buyers see value in current pricing and availability. It also indicates that trade participants are beginning to position themselves for the transition into the next crop year, a pattern that often accelerates as harvest approaches.

Total commitments now stand at 438.26 million lbs., up 8.9% versus the 402.31 million lbs. committed at this point in the prior season. Uncommitted inventory, meanwhile, has declined to 469.54 million lbs., down 5.2% from the 495.22 million lbs. that remained uncommitted through May 2025. Together, these figures paint a picture of a market that is moving supply more efficiently and with greater forward visibility than at the comparable point last crop year, a constructive signal as the industry looks ahead to harvest.

## In a Nutshell

The May 2026 data tell a story of a crop year that has performed close to expectations without delivering dramatic surprises in either direction. Year-to-date receipts are running only slightly behind last season, monthly shipments showed positive momentum, and the cumulative shipment shortfall remains contained at 2.2%. The most encouraging development is the strength of new sales, which at 21.5% above last May's pace reflects a meaningful uptick in buyer engagement at this stage of the season.

The decline in uncommitted inventory is perhaps the clearest indicator of improving market health. With 469.54 million lbs. still available and the season approaching its close, the trade has less unallocated supply to work through than it did at this time last year, and the growth in committed positions provides a firmer floor for overall movement. Handlers and exporters will be focused on converting that committed volume into shipped pounds before the crop year concludes.

Looking ahead, the industry's attention will turn quickly to the incoming 2026 crop, including early estimates of crop size, water availability, and how new-crop pricing will align with buyer demand. The relative tightness in uncommitted inventory this season, combined with the strength of forward sales, suggests that the market enters harvest season on reasonably stable footing. While uncertainties around trade policy and global economic conditions remain, the fundamentals visible in this report offer a measured degree of confidence heading into the next phase of the crop year.

## Additional Insights

The May 2026 position data reflects a market navigating a cautious but gradually stabilizing demand environment. While cumulative shipments for the 2025–26 crop year remain modestly below the prior year's pace, the month-of-May rebound — with shipments up 2.6% year-over-year — suggests handlers are beginning to move volume more aggressively as the marketing year enters its final stretch. The notably strong forward commitments, up 8.9% year-over-year at 438.26 million pounds, are an encouraging signal that buyers, particularly in key export markets across India, the European Union, and the Middle East, are locking in supply ahead of the new crop transition. The Almond Board of California has continued to emphasize demand-building efforts in emerging and high-growth markets, and those programs appear to be contributing to resilience in export commitments despite macroeconomic headwinds including currency pressures in several key markets. Competing origins, notably Australia, have seen Select Harvest and other growers report a solid 2025–26 southern hemisphere harvest, which may have modestly pressured California's share in certain Asian markets during the first half of the crop year.
