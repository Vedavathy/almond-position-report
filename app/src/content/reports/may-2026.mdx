## Market Update

The ABC released the May 2026 Position Report on Thursday, June 11th, 2026. Covering the period August 1, 2025 through May 31, 2026, this is the tenth and second-to-last monthly report for the 2025 crop year.

## Receipts

Receipts in May were 2.53 million lbs., down 10.1% from last May's 2.81 million lbs. YTD receipts stand at 2.691 billion lbs., down 0.77% vs. the 2024 crop's 2.712 billion lbs. at the same point. Receipts are now effectively complete; historically, May-through-July receipts represent less than 0.3% of total crop. The 2025 crop is on track to finish at approximately 2.691 billion lbs., about 21 million lbs. below the 2024 crop.

## Shipments

Total shipments for the month were 217.36 million lbs., up 2.6% versus last May's 211.76 million lbs., the first year-over-year monthly gain of the 2025 crop year. Export shipments drove the improvement, rising 5.2% to 169.11 million lbs. from 160.73 million lbs. a year ago. Domestic shipments remained soft at 48.25 million lbs., down 5.5% from 51.03 million lbs. last May.

YTD shipments stand at 2.213 billion lbs., down 2.2% vs. 2.263 billion lbs. for the 2024 crop at the same point. Domestic YTD shipments of 492.31 million lbs. are down 13.6% year-over-year, reflecting persistent weakness in U.S. demand throughout the crop year. Export YTD shipments of 1.721 billion lbs. are up 1.7% versus the prior year.

Regional dynamics were varied. Vietnam continued its standout performance at 36% above last year on a YTD basis with strong absorption of shelled product. Belgium and Western Europe broadly tracked up approximately 3% YTD, with Belgium surging 83% YTD. Central and Eastern Europe posted 26% YTD outperformance driven by Turkey at 42% FTM and regional food processing demand. India YTD shipments of 310.07 million lbs. remained down 9% from 342.44 million lbs., though May showed early signs of recovery as stocks moved through local markets. UAE shipments fell 27% FTM, reflecting continued regional uncertainty, with low stock levels suggesting potential catch-up purchasing in June and July.

## Sales and Commitments

Sales in May were 124.94 million lbs., up 21.5% versus last May's 102.80 million lbs. Sales have slowed from the January through March pace as buyers reassess commitments ahead of the new crop season and 2026 crop size clarity.

Commitments for the 2025 crop year stand at 438.26 million lbs., up 8.9% from 402.31 million lbs. for the 2024 crop at the same point. The combined shipped and committed position is 2.651 billion lbs., representing approximately 85.0% of total supply, above last year's 83.8% at the same point.

Uncommitted 2025 crop inventory stands at 469.54 million lbs., down 5.2% from 495.22 million lbs. a year ago. The tighter uncommitted position, combined with a smaller projected 2025 crop, supports a constructive near-term pricing outlook.

## In a Nutshell

May delivered the long-awaited first year-over-year shipment gain of the crop year, led by export strength and improving demand across Southeast Asia and Central/Eastern Europe. However, domestic demand and India continue to weigh on YTD figures.

With only June and July remaining, the industry needs approximately 370 to 390 million lbs. in additional shipments to close the year within the forecasted carryout range of 500 to 550 million lbs. The 83.8% sold position is slightly below last year's 85.5% and the five-year average, suggesting the market will be active in the final two months.

The 2025 crop is effectively complete at 2.691 billion lbs. As focus shifts to the 2026 crop, the removal of the NASS Objective Estimate eliminates a key source of late-season uncertainty, a factor that contributed to July 2025's price volatility. This structural change supports a more measured purchasing environment heading into the summer.

## 2026 Crop Outlook

Market attention has increasingly shifted toward the 2026 crop. The USDA Subjective Estimate released May 12, 2026 placed the 2026 crop at 2.70 billion lbs., flat to both the 2024 and 2025 crops, and in line with industry consensus.

Bearing acreage declined for the first time since 1995, with Land IQ reporting 1,385,870 acres, down over 15,000 acres from 2025. Total almond acreage has declined for four consecutive years. Blue Diamond Growers estimates 2.69 billion lbs. (range: 2.675 to 2.72 billion lbs.), citing water pressures and stressed orchards as downside risks. With the NASS Objective Estimate discontinued, the Subjective forecast is now the sole official USDA figure for the season.

## Additional Insights

Almond prices have improved as supply tightened and 2026 crop expectations moderated near the three-billion-pound range. Sellers have successfully pushed prices higher, though sustainability depends on continued export demand, particularly from India and China.

Poor weather during Australia's 2026 harvest reduced high-quality inshell availability for India and China, redirecting Chinese buyers back to the U.S. for supply normally sourced from Australia. This is a net positive for U.S. handlers and supports inshell premiums.

U.S. handlers shelled more product earlier in the crop year, and inshell availability, particularly Nonpareil, is constrained. With China returning to U.S. supply and India still below last year's pace, both markets need to accelerate purchases in June and July.

With the NASS Objective Estimate no longer published, the market is navigating on the Subjective Estimate alone. The removal of this data point reduces the risk of a surprise that caused the July 2025 price crash, which may encourage more progressive purchasing activity.
