## Market Update

The Almond Board of California released the May 2026 Almond Position Report on June 10, 2026. This report represents the ninth position report of the 2025–2026 crop year, covering shipment and receipt activity through the end of May 2026 and providing an updated snapshot of where the industry stands as the current crop year moves into its final months.

## Receipts

For-the-month receipts in May came in at 2.53 million lbs., down 10.1% compared to the 2.81 million lbs. recorded in May of last year. At this late stage of the crop year, monthly receipt volumes are naturally modest, reflecting the tail end of handler deliveries from the 2025 harvest rather than any structural shift in supply. Year-to-date receipts through May reached 2.691 billion lbs., down 0.8% versus the 2.712 billion lbs. posted through the same period last crop year. The narrow gap between this year and last on a cumulative basis suggests that the 2025 California crop came in meaningfully close to the prior season, with only a slight reduction in overall volume flowing into the supply chain.

## Shipments

Total for-the-month shipments in May 2026 reached 217.36 million lbs., up 2.6% compared to the 211.76 million lbs. shipped in May 2025. That improvement is an encouraging sign that demand remains active heading into the summer months, a period when buyers are typically building inventory ahead of the fall and holiday seasons. On a year-to-date basis, however, cumulative shipments through May stand at 2.213 billion lbs., which is down 2.2% from the 2.263 billion lbs. moved through the same point in the prior crop year. The monthly gain partially offsets the cumulative shortfall, though the industry will need continued shipment momentum through June and July to close that gap before the new crop arrives.

Domestic demand has contributed to the monthly improvement, with processors and retailers continuing to work through inventory in a measured way. Consumer appetite for almonds in snacking, baking, and plant-based applications remains a reliable foundation for the domestic channel. The strength seen in May's domestic numbers reflects a market that, while not accelerating dramatically, is holding its ground in a competitive snack and ingredient landscape.

On the export side, international buyers have remained engaged, and the month-over-month uptick in total shipments reflects steady pull from key overseas markets. Europe and Asia continue to represent the core of California almond export volume, and any shifts in currency dynamics or regional confectionery and dairy-alternative demand will bear watching through the balance of the crop year. The cumulative YTD export picture is softer than last year, which is consistent with broader trade patterns seen earlier in the season, but May's performance suggests that pace is stabilizing rather than deteriorating further.

## Sales and Commitments

New sales activity in May was a clear bright spot in this report, coming in at 124.94 million lbs., up 21.5% compared to the 102.80 million lbs. in new sales recorded during May 2025. That is a meaningful increase and indicates that buyers are actively contracting for near-term and forward needs with more conviction than they showed at the same point last year. Whether this reflects improved price competitiveness, stronger end-user demand signals, or both, the 21.5% gain reinforces that the market's commercial foundation is in solid shape as the crop year winds down.

Total commitments as of the end of May stood at 438.26 million lbs., up 8.9% from the 402.31 million lbs. committed through May 2025. Uncommitted inventory, meanwhile, declined to 469.54 million lbs., down 5.2% from the 495.22 million lbs. that remained uncommitted at this time last year. A smaller pool of uncommitted product paired with growing commitments tells a constructive story for price stability and orderly market clearing ahead of the new crop. With roughly two months remaining before the 2026 harvest begins arriving, the industry appears to be in a reasonably well-balanced position.

## In a Nutshell

The May 2026 position report presents a nuanced but generally encouraging picture of the California almond market. Monthly shipments turned positive year-over-year, new sales activity surged by more than one-fifth compared to last May, and uncommitted inventory is running below the prior year's level. These factors together suggest that the trade is engaged, price discovery is functioning, and the channel is not carrying excess unsold product into the new crop season.

The modest YTD shipment shortfall of 2.2% relative to last year is worth monitoring but should be viewed in context. The prior crop year set a high baseline for comparison, and the pace of improvement seen in May suggests that gap may continue to narrow. Receipts on a YTD basis are nearly flat with last year, confirming that supply is not materially different in scale, which means demand-side execution will be the determining factor in how cleanly this crop clears before the next harvest.

Looking ahead, the industry's ability to sustain the sales momentum demonstrated in May will be critical. Buyers are showing a willingness to commit, uncommitted inventory is at a manageable level, and global demand channels are active even if not uniformly robust. As growers and handlers turn their attention to the incoming 2026 crop, the market enters that transition from a position of relative balance rather than strain, which is the most constructive posture the industry can hope for at this stage of the crop year.

## Additional Market Insights

**Australian Crop Shortfall Redirects Asian Demand**
The 2025–26 Australian almond harvest, concentrated in the Riverland and Sunraysia regions, came in below initial projections following late-season heat stress during February and March 2026 pollination and fill periods. Reduced availability from Australia — historically a competitive supplier to Southeast Asian and South Asian markets during the Northern Hemisphere off-season — is supporting incremental demand for California supply in those channels, a dynamic bullish for U.S. export commitments and near-term price floors heading into the June–August shipping window.

**India Re-Entry Signal Strengthens Commitment Pipeline**
Indian import activity, which softened through the December–February period amid rupee pressure and cautious post-Diwali inventory management, shows renewed buying interest as of late April and May 2026, consistent with seasonal re-stocking ahead of the monsoon-period demand trough. The rupee's partial stabilization against the U.S. dollar in Q1 2026 has improved landed cost economics for Indian importers, and trade sources indicate forward cover being established at levels not seen since mid-2025 — bullish for committed positions and supportive of the 8.9% YOY commitment increase already reflected in the ABC data through non-overlapping buyer channels.

**USDA Forecast Signals Tighter 2026–27 Supply Transition**
USDA's May 2026 World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE) and preliminary California tree nut outlook suggest the 2026–27 California almond crop is trending toward a modest year-over-year decline relative to the 2025–26 bearing acreage baseline, reflecting continued orchard removals driven by sustained water cost pressures in the San Joaquin Valley and grower profitability constraints at recent price levels. If realized, a smaller forward crop would shift the fundamental balance toward tighter uncommitted inventory by Q1 2027, a structurally bullish signal for handlers considering the pace at which current uncommitted positions are sold down over the summer and fall months.

**NASS Objective Estimate Discontinuation Creates Forecast Uncertainty**
USDA's National Agricultural Statistics Service discontinued the Almond Objective Measurement report series, removing an independent mid-season production benchmark that the trade historically used to calibrate positions against ABC projections. In its absence, crop size uncertainty is higher than in prior years, and handlers and end-users are likely to price in a wider risk premium on forward contracts — a structural development that may compress willingness to commit on long-dated basis contracts while simultaneously supporting spot price volatility as the August–September harvest approaches.

**Chinese Bulk Demand Remains Selectively Cautious**
Chinese almond import activity in the January–April 2026 period reflects continued preference for processed and value-added formats over raw kernel, consistent with a multi-year structural shift in consumer demand and ongoing domestic economic caution among Chinese food manufacturers managing margin pressure. While container-level spot buying has been episodic rather than sustained, trade sources note that several major Chinese importers are monitoring California price levels for opportunistic Q3 purchasing — a conditionally bullish signal contingent on price discovery in the June–July window, but not yet a confirmed demand recovery.

**Euro Strength Supports European Buying Capacity**
The euro's appreciation against the U.S. dollar through H1 2026, tracking broader dollar softness related to U.S. fiscal and trade policy uncertainty, has improved European buyers' landed cost position for California almonds denominated in dollars. Spain's domestic almond crop, while not yet fully assessed for 2026 harvest, faces ongoing drought pressure in Castilla-La Mancha and Extremadura that may limit domestic availability and push Spanish processors toward California raw material — a combination of currency tailwind and competing-origin supply pressure that is net bullish for U.S. export volumes to Europe in the August–December 2026 forward period.
