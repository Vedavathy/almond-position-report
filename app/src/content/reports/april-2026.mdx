## Market Update

The ABC released the April Position Report on Tuesday, May 12th. This is the ninth report for the 2025 crop year.

## Receipts

Receipts in April were 3.90 million lbs., down 52.3% from last April's receipts of 8.17 million lbs. YTD receipts total 2.69 billion lbs., which is just under Crop Year 2024 YTD receipts of 2.708 billion lbs. Historically, receipts through April represent 99.7% of total crop receipts. Based on this trend, the 2025 crop is expected to finish near 2.695 billion lbs.

## Shipments

Total shipments FTM were 219.90 million lbs., coming in under industry expectations of approximately 225 million lbs. April shipments were down 8.8% versus last April's 241.10 million lbs. YTD shipments currently stand at 1.776 billion lbs., down 1.9% vs. April shipments last year of 2.051 billion lbs. Market activity remained cautious due to ongoing Middle East tensions and buyer hesitation ahead of multiple 2026 crop estimate releases.

Domestic shipments FTM were 52.62 million lbs., down 1.7% vs. last year's 53.30 million lbs. YTD domestic shipments currently stand at 444.06 million lbs., down 14.4% vs last year's shipments of 518.84 million lbs. Domestic demand has been a consistent weak point throughout the crop year, driving the YTD shortfall.

Export shipments FTM were 167.27 million lbs., which is down 10.9% vs. last year's 187.81 million lbs., which was the largest April export shipment month in recorded history. YTD export shipments currently stand at 1.552 billion lbs., up 1.28% vs last year's YTD shipments of 1.532 billion lbs. The 5 year average of April export shipments is 173.51 million lbs., which aligns closer to export shipments we saw in April of this year. Regional dynamics were mixed: Shipments to the UAE declined sharply for the second consecutive month due to ongoing regional instability, with Turkey continuing to absorb much of the displaced volume. The EU and Vietnam softened after strong March shipments but remain ahead on a YTD basis. India shipped 1,002 containers in April, 456 fewer than the same month in 2025, keeping India behind last year's pace YTD. April shipments to India declined by 14.4 million lbs. compared with April 2025. Total export shipments for the month were down 20.5 million lbs. year over year, with India representing approximately 70% of the overall reduction in April's export volume.

## Sales and Commitments

Sales FTM were 160.70 million lbs., down 16.5% vs. last year's sales of 192.55 million lbs. Sales activity slowed in April following a strong January through March period. With multiple 2026 crop estimates released throughout April and May, buyers hesitated on new commitments as they evaluated the projections and assessed how the market may trend heading into the tail-end of the crop year.

Commitments for the 2025 crop year stand at 516.87 million lbs., down 1.6% vs the 525.07 million lbs. from last year.

Uncommitted 2025 crop inventory sits at 605.81 million lbs. which is up 4.2% vs. last year's 581.46 million lbs.

## In a Nutshell

The April position report introduces added complexity to a market that had trended bullish over the past month. Shipments and sales fell short of expectations, but receipts remain on track to finish between 2.69 and 2.70 billion lbs., below the 2025 crop of 2.712 billion lbs. If May through July shipments match 2024 levels, and assuming a 2.695 billion lb. crop with a 2% loss and exempt, projected carryout is approximately 534 million lbs., about 10% above last year. Under this same crop assumption, the industry is 80.1% sold on total supply, in line with the five year average of 80.5%.

Market focus over the past month has centered on 2026 crop size. With the USDA/NASS Objective Estimate discontinued, the industry is relying on five separate estimates released in April and May to gauge 2026 crop production potential.

Land IQ reported 1.385 million bearing acres, a decline of roughly 15,000 acres from 2025 and the first reduction in bearing acreage in 30 years. A key discussion point is the amount of abandoned bearing acres, which are not included in Land IQ's initial estimate and will be captured in their final November acreage report.

Global supply dynamics are also shifting. Australia's crop, historically a competitive source of inshell almonds for India and China, has been affected by poor weather during harvest which had reduced availability of high quality inshell. This has pushed China back to the U.S. for supply typically sourced from Australia. At the same time, many U.S. handlers report to have shelled more product early in the crop year, tightening expected inshell availability. Nonpareil is particularly constrained, with receipts through April 68 million lbs. behind last year. With China returning to the U.S. and India behind last year's shipment pace, both regions will need to accelerate coverage due to constrained inshell supply.

Prior to this week's reports, lower than expected crop estimates supported firmer bids and higher offer levels, though concerns about demand sustainability at elevated prices kept trading focused on prompt needs. With the Subjective Estimate now broadly aligned with other projections, California sellers are pushing for higher prices. While transactions have occurred at these elevated levels to cover near term demand, the sustainability of pricing will ultimately depend on continued global demand, which remains uncertain. The weaker than expected April position report, particularly in sales and shipments, adds further complexity to the outlook. The INC Congress in Macau is underway, and buyer-seller engagement is expected to increase following the release of both the Subjective Estimate and the April report.

The market is expected to remain range bound, as the likelihood of a smaller 2026 crop is countered by weaker April shipments and sales.
